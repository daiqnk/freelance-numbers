Etsy Fee & Profit Calculator: How Listing, Transaction and Ads Fees Stack

Updated 16 October 2026 · Not affiliated with or endorsed by Etsy, Inc. Fee rates below are illustrative examples for US sellers — always check Etsy's current fee pages. How we make content

A $28 sale on Etsy can leave you a lot less after listing fees, the transaction fee, payment processing, optional Offsite Ads, materials, shipping and your time. This guide breaks down how those fees stack, with a worked example, then points to free and paid spreadsheets that do the math for you.

The main Etsy selling fees (examples)

For many US sellers using Etsy Payments, the core fees look like this (illustrative):

You may also see advertising (Etsy Ads), Pattern, or other optional fees. Always confirm your own rates in your seller account and on Etsy's fee pages.

Worked example: a $28 item

Illustrative only. Assume a US seller, one item at $28, buyer pays $5 shipping, no gift wrap, no sales tax on the fee base, and the sale is not from Offsite Ads. Listing fee for this sale is treated as $0.20 (one listing used up by the sale).

Item + shipping received$28 + $5$33.00
Listing feeflat$0.20

If the same order were attributed to Offsite Ads at 15%, that alone would be about $4.95 on the $33 order total — often more than the other fees combined. That is why channel and ad attribution matter when you judge profit per sale.

From fee total to real profit

Fees are only part of the picture. Real profit per unit also needs:

  • Materials (and a bill of materials if one product uses several supplies)
  • Packaging and shipping cost you pay (labels, postage, mailers) — separate from what the buyer paid you for shipping
  • Your labor at an hourly rate you choose (even if you don't "pay" yourself yet)
  • Discounts, refunds, and other expenses (ads, tools, market fees)
Net profit ≈ (price + shipping charged)
           − Etsy fees
           − materials − packaging − shipping you paid
           − labor (hours × your rate)
           − other expenses allocated to the sale

A sale can look fine on the order screen and still lose money once materials and time are included.

Pricing for a target margin

Work backwards. Pick a target margin after fees and costs (for example 40% of the item price, or a fixed dollar profit). Then solve for the list price that hits it, given your fee rates and costs. Spreadsheets do this with editable fee cells so you can update rates when Etsy changes them.

Not tax or accounting advice. Sales tax, VAT and income tax rules depend on where you and your buyers are. Keep proper records and ask a qualified professional when needed.
