The Schedule E expense lines

Schedule E (Form 1040), Part I is where most individual landlords report rental income and expenses for each property. The expense lines are:

LineCategoryExamples of what goes here
5AdvertisingListing-site fees, signs, ads for a vacancy
6Auto and travelMileage or actual car costs to collect rent or maintain the property; travel away from home whose main purpose is managing the rental (see our mileage page)
7Cleaning and maintenanceTurnover cleaning, lawn care, pest control
8CommissionsLeasing or tenant-placement commissions
9InsuranceLandlord/fire policy for the year (prepaid multi-year premiums are spread over the years covered)
10Legal and other professional feesLease review, eviction attorney, tax prep for the Schedule E part
11Management feesProperty manager's monthly percentage
12Mortgage interest paid to banks, etc.Interest from Form 1098 (interest only, never principal)
13Other interestInterest on other loans used for the rental
14RepairsFixing what's broken without bettering, restoring or adapting the property (see repairs vs improvements)
15SuppliesLight bulbs, smoke-alarm batteries, cleaning supplies
16TaxesProperty tax on the rental
17UtilitiesUtilities you pay as the landlord
18Depreciation expense or depletionYearly depreciation of the building and improvements (see the depreciation calculator)
19OtherHOA/condo dues for maintenance of common areas, equipment rental, other ordinary and necessary costs (list them)

The examples column is our plain-English summary; the official wording is in the Schedule E instructions linked below.

Three rules that trip people up

  • Mortgage principal is not an expense. Pub. 527 says you can't simply deduct mortgage or principal payments; the building's cost is recovered through depreciation instead.
  • Improvements aren't repairs. An expense that results in a betterment, restoration, or adaptation of the property must be capitalized and depreciated.
  • Insurance paid in advance is spread. If you pay a premium for more than one year in advance, you deduct only the part that applies to each year.

How to set up your bookkeeping categories

Use exactly these line names as the category list in your spreadsheet or bank-feed rules, one column per property. Two extra non-expense columns help: capital improvements (to depreciate later) and security deposits held (not income while you plan to return them). At year-end, a pivot by line number gives you each Schedule E figure.

Quick answers

What expense categories does Schedule E use for rental property?

Lines 5 to 19 of Schedule E, Part I: advertising, auto and travel, cleaning and maintenance, commissions, insurance, legal and other professional fees, management fees, mortgage interest paid to banks, other interest, repairs, supplies, taxes, utilities, depreciation, and other.

Can I deduct my mortgage payment on a rental?

Only the interest part (line 12). IRS Publication 527 says you can't deduct mortgage principal payments; the building cost is recovered through depreciation.
