Repairs vs Improvements on a Rental Property: Deduct Now or Depreciate?

Checked 2026-10-16 against IRS Publication 527 (2025) and the other IRS pages listed under Sources. US federal rules only. How we make content

A $4,000 repair and a $4,000 improvement look identical on your bank statement, but the tax treatment is very different: one is deducted this year, the other is spread over 27.5 years for a residential building. Here is how IRS Publication 527 draws the line.

The test: betterment, restoration or adaptation

Pub. 527 says you must capitalize any expense that improves your rental property. An expense is an improvement if it results in:

  • Betterment: fixing a pre-existing defect, enlarging or expanding the property, or increasing its capacity, strength or quality.
  • Restoration: replacing a substantial structural part, repairing damage after a casualty basis adjustment, or rebuilding to like-new condition.
  • Adaptation: altering the property to a use that isn't consistent with its ordinary use when you began renting it.

If none of these applies, a cost of repairing or maintaining the property can generally be deducted.

Examples the IRS lists as improvements

Table 1-1 in Pub. 527 lists, among others: additions (bedroom, bathroom, deck, garage, porch, patio); landscaping, driveway, walkway, fence, retaining wall, sprinkler system, swimming pool; storm windows and doors, a new roof, wiring upgrades, security system; heating system, central air, furnace, duct work; septic system, water heater, soft-water and filtration systems; built-in appliances, kitchen modernization, flooring, wall-to-wall carpeting, insulation.

Two safe harbors worth knowing

Pub. 527 points to two elections under the tangible property regulations: the de minimis safe harbor (lets you deduct certain small acquisition costs on line 19 instead of capitalizing) and the routine maintenance safe harbor (may let you deduct a cost even if it looks like an improvement). The conditions and dollar limits are in the IRS FAQ linked below; check them before relying on either.

Keep the records separate

Pub. 527 advises separating repair and improvement costs and keeping accurate records, because you need improvement costs when you depreciate or sell. In a spreadsheet, give improvements their own tab with date placed in service and cost, and keep repairs in the normal expense log on Schedule E line 14.

Quick answers

Is a new roof on a rental a repair or an improvement?

IRS Publication 527, Table 1-1, lists a new roof as an improvement, so it is capitalized and depreciated, generally over the same 27.5-year recovery period as the residential building.

What makes a rental expense an improvement?

Pub. 527 says an expense is an improvement if it results in a betterment to the property, restores the property, or adapts it to a new or different use.
