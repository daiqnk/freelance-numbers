How much to charge as a freelance writer

Wage data: U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, OEWS May 2025 (SOC 27-3043), retrieved 2026-10-16. Our calculations and assumptions are labelled. How we make content

Employed writers and authors in the US earn a median $36.98 an hour ($76,910 a year), according to the BLS. A freelancer who wants the same pay has to cover unbilled hours, business costs and the employer half of payroll taxes, which with the assumptions below works out to roughly $70.32 an hour, about 1.9× the employee median.

* Hourly × 2,080, the full-time year the BLS uses for annual figures. The category covers people who write original content for books, websites, advertising, scripts and other media. OEWS counts wage and salary employees only; it does not survey the self-employed, and its wages exclude benefits. So this is a benchmark for what an employer would pay, not a survey of freelance rates.

From employee wage to freelance rate

An employee is paid for about 2,080 hours a year. A freelancer only bills some of their hours; the rest goes to finding clients, admin, invoicing and unpaid gaps. Our formula:

hourly rate = (target income × 1.0648 + business costs + benefits you fund)
              ÷ billable hours ÷ (1 − payment fee %)
  • 1.0648: self-employment tax is 15.3% (IRS), applied to 92.35% of net earnings, which is 14.13%; an employee pays 7.65% themselves, so the extra is about 6.48%. This is an approximation; it ignores the Social Security wage cap and the income-tax deduction for half of SE tax.
  • Business costs: we use $6,000/year as an example (software, equipment, insurance, accounting). This is our assumption, not a statistic. Use your own.
  • Billable hours: we show 1,000, 1,250 and 1,500 hours a year as scenarios. Again, assumptions: count your own.

Turning the hourly floor into quotes

Many freelance writers and authors price per word, per article or per project; convert any per-word offer back to an hourly figure before accepting. Whatever the unit, estimate the hours behind it and check the quote against your floor with the project quote calculator.

Tip: Track how many words you actually finish per hour, including research and edits, so per-word offers can be checked against your floor.
